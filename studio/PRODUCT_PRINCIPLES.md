# Blackglass product principles

1. Make the user's next useful action clear. Visual impact must support a working experience.
2. Preserve useful content, data, working forms and existing capabilities; never erase them to simplify a redesign.
3. Treat mobile as a primary environment. Check real viewport constraints, touch targets and returning/error states.
4. Preserve the defining interaction in the approved brief. Record meaningful engineering trade-offs against the brief. Routine reversible choices inside its approved scope may proceed; escalate changes that materially alter the promised experience or risk.
5. Use motion to communicate state, continuity, hierarchy or physicality. Keep core actions usable with reduced motion.
6. Make claims and availability truthful. Do not invent outcomes, testimonials, credentials, usage numbers or released features.
7. Keep customer, health, financial, enquiry and authentication data out of public commits and review evidence. Use synthetic fixtures.
8. Isolate experiments until reviewed. A successful prototype is evidence to consider, not permission to replace production code or move hosting.
9. Test the result against the brief, functionality, accessibility and device constraints. An untested state is not a pass.
10. Separate implementation, verification, merge and publication. Each has its own evidence and approval boundary.

Use these alongside the approved task brief and [source/release boundaries](collaboration/CURRENT_STATE.md), not in place of them.
